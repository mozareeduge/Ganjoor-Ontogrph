## 1. Research situation and contribution

A researcher notices that an object keeps returning in different poetic situations. A mirror appears with rust, polishing, obscuration, reflection, or breakage. A candle appears through melting, cutting, smoke, light, and duration. A bird meets cages, corpses, branches, messengers, or divine names. The first question is not yet what the object *means*. The researcher does not know whether these recurrences form one convention, several local formations, a pattern concentrated in one poet, a transformation across corpus regions, or partly an effect of the search procedure itself.

Ganjoor makes this problem researchable at scales that close reading alone cannot survey. Its static data exposes poets, hierarchical categories, poems, sections, verses, verse order, metre, rhyme, and source metadata. Yet scale introduces another reduction. A query may return hundreds of matches without showing what those passages are doing. A co-incidence can be driven almost entirely by one poet. A relation that looks dense at poem scale can collapse at couplet scale. A summary can make one aspect of a poem easy to retrieve while suppressing another. The research therefore needs a reversible movement between corpus-scale construction and exact poetic situations.

**Ganjoor Ontograph** is specified as that research apparatus. It lets a researcher construct a declared fraction of Ganjoor, follow a provisional object through explicit lexical handles, calibrate what those handles actually retrieve, produce reproducible distant-reading surfaces, return every consequential result to its source passages, preserve relations before their final description is fixed, vary the conditions of encounter, and release the current research state with its evidence, reductions, uncertainties, and reopening conditions intact.

The contribution is not an automated interpretation of Persian poetry. It is a method and software architecture for making **conditioned, replayable encounters** with a literary corpus while keeping the difference between source text, search result, research object, statistical construction, interpretation, and claim visible.

# Part I — Methodological foundations

## 2. From a search string to an object inquiry

Typing `آینه` into a search system produces a textual access route. It does not by itself establish that every match is the same research object, or even that every match should count as an occurrence of the object currently under study. A spelling variant may retrieve the same formation; a homographic or figurative use may not; a later close reading may show that the initial address has compressed several distinct objects.

This practical problem gives Object-Oriented Ontology a precise role in the project. Graham Harman's object–quality ontology treats an object as exceeding the components, relations, uses, effects, descriptions, and presentations through which it becomes available (Harman 2002; 2010; 2011; 2018). Ontograph transfers one methodological pressure from that position: **a representation can disclose an object without exhausting it**. The software therefore stores research addresses and conditioned accesses rather than pretending that a database record, graph node, statistical profile, or interpretation has captured the object as a whole.

Harman's fourfold distinguishes Real Objects (RO), Sensual Objects (SO), Real Qualities (RQ), and Sensual Qualities (SQ), with the technical tensions Time, Space, Eidos, and Essence (Harman 2010; 2011). These terms enter only when object identity or quality becomes load-bearing. They are not aliases for chronology, geography, hidden metadata, or ordinary classification.

### 2.1 Research attention is selective

Ontological parity does not determine research priority. A poem, poet, mirror, comma, database row, server process, metre record, and interface state can all be treated as objects without receiving equal attention in a particular inquiry. The **Field Charter** makes that practical asymmetry explicit through inclusion, exclusion, scale, and reopening rules.

### 2.2 Evidential gaps remain evidential gaps

When data is missing, inaccessible, transformed, or uncertain, the apparatus records that condition directly. Missing metadata is handled as missing metadata; an inaccessible source is handled through provenance and uncertainty; a hidden field remains technical opacity. These conditions do not become Harmanian withdrawal, a Real Object, or a Real Quality merely because access is limited. The philosophical restraint keeps an encounter open; it does not turn ordinary research limitations into metaphysical evidence.

## 3. Ontographic arrangement before explanatory hierarchy

A corpus inquiry needs to decide what can be placed together before it decides what those things ultimately mean. Ian Bogost's **ontography** is useful at this moment because it makes heterogeneous units available in arrangements—lists, exploded views, compendia, and constructed machines—without requiring one explanatory hierarchy to absorb them first (Bogost 2012, 35–60).

Ontograph transfers that operation into corpus research. A field can hold poets, poems, verses, lexical anchors, material objects named in poems, metres, places, dates, sources, summaries, mappings, and researcher-made relations, while keeping their roles distinct. Their later importance is established by evidence and research consequences, not by the fact that they entered the inventory.

Bogost's **metaphorism** and **carpentry** enter for different reasons. Metaphorism treats access to another unit as necessarily indirect; carpentry treats construction as a way of thinking through a problem by making an apparatus that encounters it differently (Bogost 2012, 61–112). In this project, neither practice licenses free analogy. An interpretive or technical transfer earns methodological authority only when the source operation, transformation, consequence, and remainder can be inspected.

## 4. Disciplined Metaphoring and bridge records

**Disciplined Metaphoring** is the project's local operation for a deliberate transfer across non-equivalent objects, domains, media, or registers. It records what is being transferred, what must change in the target register, what does not transfer, and what concrete research or design consequence follows (Zare 2026a).

This matters when a philosophical pressure becomes software design. “An object exceeds its present access,” for example, does not become a database class called `RealObject`. It creates a research requirement: several conditioned accesses must be able to coexist without overwriting one another. That requirement creates a design decision: preserve contrastive **Profiles**. The design decision then creates a technical consequence: Profiles need versioned records linked to their source, apparatus, transformations, limits, and authority.

Whenever a cross-domain transfer materially shapes the architecture, a **Bridge Record** stores:

1. source concept or operation;
2. source sense;
3. target research pressure;
4. invariant being transferred;
5. transformations required by the target;
6. remainder or non-transfer;
7. design consequence;
8. technical consequence;
9. failure condition.

A bridge record is therefore a traceability device, not evidence that source and target are identical.

## 5. Vocabulary provenance

The project uses three vocabularies. Their provenance remains visible because theoretical precision and implementation precision depend on different kinds of authority.

| Vocabulary | Terms used here | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Harman | object non-exhaustion/withdrawal; RO, SO, RQ, SQ; Time, Space, Eidos, Essence; allure; confrontation; vicarious/indirect causation | source-technical philosophical terms (Harman 2005; 2010; 2011; 2018) |
| Bogost | ontography, metaphorism, carpentry, wonder, ontographic machines, relevant uses of unit operations | source-technical practices (Bogost 2006; 2012) |

The local terms earn their place by preserving a consequential distinction, changing a research decision, enabling a test, or making later reconstruction possible. They are not retrospective definitions of Harman or Bogost.

# Part II — Research method and study records

## 6. Create the Research Situation Record

A study begins when a concrete recurrence, discrepancy, resistance, absence, material behavior, or failed interpretation makes the current account insufficient. The **Research Situation Record** preserves that beginning before a thesis absorbs it.

Record:

- what appeared or differed;
- through which source, witness, corpus operation, or encounter it appeared;
- why it became consequential;
- the first question it generated;
- what remains uncertain;
- what decision would be premature at this point.

For mirror, a valid starting situation might be: several remembered passages couple reflective surfaces with polishing, rust, obscuration, or breakage; the researcher wants to know which material contacts recur, where they are concentrated, and whether the lexical category “mirror” survives close inspection as one research object.

**Output:** `ResearchSituation`.

**Move on when:** the inquiry can be justified from the material pressure itself rather than from the thesis it may later support.

## 7. Construct the Object-Field

An **Object-Field** is a versioned arrangement of heterogeneous addressable entities held together for one declared inquiry. Its first function is scope control: another researcher should be able to reconstruct why a poem, poet, category, date proxy, lexical condition, or other entity entered the study and what would cause that boundary to change.

Every field begins with a **Field Charter** recording:

- research purpose;
- corpus snapshot;
- inclusion and exclusion rules;
- active encounter scales;
- empirical temporal scope;
- empirical spatial or institutional scope when relevant;
- known blind zones;
- reopening conditions.

The field can later expand, contract, split, or be compared with another field. Earlier versions remain available because a boundary change may itself explain why a pattern appeared or disappeared.

## 8. Separate the research object from the mechanism that finds it

A researcher may begin by typing “mirror,” but five distinct records are required before that starting word can responsibly become an object-level count.

### Seed

A **Seed** records the initiating object or cluster as the researcher currently conceives it, together with the reason for following it and the uncertainty attached to it.

### Object Address

An **Object Address** gives provisional, persistent identity to something the study needs to return to. It can later split, merge, or become compound. Its identifier preserves research continuity without claiming exhaustive representation.

### Lexical Anchor

A **Lexical Anchor** records a textual form used to locate candidate passages. For mirror, the first set might contain `آینه` and `آیینه`, together with a named normalization and match policy.

### Anchor Hit

An **Anchor Hit** is the deterministic fact that an approved anchor matched one source span under those rules. It preserves the source text, match span, matcher, normalization profile, and corpus address.

### Occurrence Assessment

An **Occurrence Assessment** records whether a particular Anchor Hit may count as an occurrence of the current Object Address: `accepted`, `rejected`, or `ambiguous`. It carries the assessor, rationale, policy version, and uncertainty.

The resulting chain is:

**Seed → Object Address → Lexical Anchor → Anchor Hit → Occurrence Assessment**

This chain is essential in Persian poetry because spelling variation, compounds, polysemy, figurative constructions, names, quotation, grammatical context, and normalization can all make a lexical census diverge from object-level incidence.

### 8.1 Object-incidence modes

A study declares which level of this chain a calculation uses. The interface and exported result must name that mode.

| Mode | What is counted | Permitted wording |
|---|---|---|
| `anchor` | all Anchor Hits matching declared rules | exact lexical/anchor incidence |
| `assessed-full` | all hits have an Occurrence Assessment | exact count under the declared assessment policy |
| `assessed-rule` | a deterministic inclusion/exclusion rule has been validated and versioned for the study | exact count under that validated rule |
| `estimated` | a declared probability sample of hits has been assessed | estimated object incidence with uncertainty; never “complete object census” |

`assessed-rule` remains a study-specific operationalization. Its validity does not make the rule a universal semantic classifier. In `estimated` mode, the sampling frame, strata, random seed, weights/estimator, ambiguity treatment, and uncertainty interval travel with every derived result.

### 8.1.1 Ambiguous hits and the denominator

v2.2.0 stated that `ambiguous` hits are excluded from exact binary object incidence, but left open what happens to their unit in the *denominator* used by Prevalence (§27.4) — an easy silent collapse, since "excluded from the numerator" and "excluded from the eligible-unit denominator" produce different, non-interchangeable prevalence values.

The rule: a unit whose only hits are `ambiguous` (no `accepted` hit present) **remains in the eligible-unit denominator** and is scored `I(u,o)=0` for binary incidence, exactly as a unit with no hit at all would be. It is never dropped from `U_F`. Every prevalence, co-incidence, or scale-profile result computed under `assessed-full` or `assessed-rule` must additionally report the **count and share of eligible units carrying only ambiguous hits**, displayed beside the prevalence figure rather than folded into either the numerator or a silently shrunk denominator. A field where ambiguity is concentrated in one region (one poet, one source) is itself a finding candidate, not noise to be averaged away.

## 9. Calibrate before scaling

Before an object-level calculation governs a large field, **Close Calibration** pressures the current anchors and Object Address against actual passages. The calibration set combines random or stratified cases, different poets or categories, high- and low-density regions, normalization boundary cases, and ambiguous or negative examples. Top-ranked search results alone are not an adequate calibration set.

Each sampled hit opens through a context ladder: match, verse or hemistich, couplet, section, poem, category, poet, and field. The researcher records Occurrence Assessments and may revise the anchor set or split the Object Address when the sample exposes a material difference.

The calibration receipt preserves:

- sampling frame and eligibility;
- sample size and strata;
- random seed when randomness is used;
- sampled Anchor Hit IDs;
- assessments and rationale;
- proposed rule, if `assessed-rule` mode is used;
- validation result for that rule;
- unresolved ambiguity;
- resulting Object Address and Lexical Anchor versions.

Calibration does not finalize the object's ontology. It establishes the first reproducible counting policy and the conditions under which that policy must be reopened.

## 10. Preserve conditioned access through Profiles

A **Profile** records how an addressed object or relation becomes available under specified conditions. It binds the addressed entity to a source or witness, access apparatus, transformation history, available qualities, limits, authority scope, field version, and uncertainty (Zare 2026a; 2026c).

A mirror Object Address can therefore have distinct Profiles through the full field, one poet's ghazals, a couplet-scale encounter rule, normalized plain text, the source verse, an AI-generated summary, or a researcher-created Mapping Object. These are not interchangeable presentations. A transformed summary and an original verse can both be useful while supporting different claims.

A **Mapping Object** may function as an apparatus through which a Profile is produced, but the two records remain separate: the Mapping Object is the constructed arrangement; the Profile records what became available through that arrangement and under what conditions.

## 11. Keep empirical time and space empirical

Ganjoor supplies biographical dates and places when known, hierarchical corpus structure, and source metadata. These can define empirical research conditions. They do not become Harman's technical Time or Space.

A “thirteenth-century field,” for example, is only as precise as its rule. If poem-level composition dates are absent, the apparatus may construct a **poet-life chronological proxy**, such as poets whose documented life interval overlaps a declared Hijri range. The proxy rule becomes part of the Field Charter and travels with every result. Likewise, poet birth or death place cannot silently become the place where a poem was composed, circulated, or performed.

## 12. Use the fourfold only when identity or quality becomes load-bearing

Ordinary corpus enumeration does not require a fourfold worksheet. The fourfold becomes useful when the study has begun making a consequential claim about what counts as one object or which qualities belong to it.

A **Fourfold Diagnostic** can ask:

- **SO–SQ / Time:** what is being encountered as one object while presented qualities change across Profiles?
- **RO–SQ / Space:** what happens when another encounter disturbs the current identification of the object with its presented qualities?
- **SO–RQ / Eidos:** which proposed identity-bearing qualities are being treated as necessary, and do counterexamples pressure them?
- **RO–RQ / Essence:** is the study making an ontologically ambitious claim about what belongs to the object beyond current presentation, and what warrants that claim?

The diagnostic exists to change the next research action. It is not a metaphysical form that every corpus entity must fill (Harman 2010; 2011; Zare 2026c).

## 13. Preserve traces before formal relations

A **Trace** is a consequential indication that can generate another research action. It may arise from close reading, a distant calculation, contradiction between Profiles, unusual absence, repeated material operation, or a comparison that the current description cannot absorb.

Each trace records what appeared, where it appeared, the Profiles or Mapping Objects involved, alternative descriptions when available, and the next action capable of discriminating between them. Most traces may remain traces or move to residue. Cheap persistence protects the study from turning every interesting event into a formal graph relation.

## 14. Give consequential relations persistent identity

A **Relation-Object** is created when a developing relation has become consequential enough to require persistent research addressability. It can be revisited, versioned, challenged, compared, split, and given permissions before its final predicate is settled.

A Relation-Object records:

- participants;
- initiating traces and encounters;
- Profiles through which it appears;
- current and alternative descriptions;
- empirical direction where relevant;
- evidence and counter-evidence;
- Mapping Objects that expose it;
- experiments performed on it;
- Use-Status and Claim Permission;
- history;
- residue and unresolved questions.

A `mirror ↔ rust` relation can therefore persist while its description changes from repeated lexical proximity to a narrower material-degradation relation in one corpus fraction. Persistent identity does not establish that every technical relation record is a new Real Object in Harman's metaphysics.

## 15. Vary encounter conditions deliberately

An **Experiment** changes one or more encounter conditions because the resulting difference may discriminate between competing descriptions. It is an operation, not another name for a Profile.

Examples include narrowing from poem to couplet, removing one dominant poet, changing an anchor set, comparing category subtrees, separating vocalized and normalized access, removing one highly connected mediator, comparing a source poem with its summary Profile, or changing a chronological proxy.

Every Experiment states before execution:

- the research pressure motivating it;
- the baseline Profile(s);
- the condition being changed;
- conditions being held stable;
- the difference that would matter;
- what would count as failure to discriminate;
- the operations and software versions used.

It may then produce new Profiles, Mapping Objects, Traces, and Findings.

## 16. Record Findings and control how claims travel

A **Finding** is an observation together with the consequence it creates for the inquiry. Its working grammar is:

**pressure → operation/construction → encounter → observation → consequence → limit**.

If removing one poet eliminates 82% of a poem-scale `mirror–rust` co-incidence, the Finding is not “Poet X caused the relation.” The defensible consequence is narrower: the apparent relation is strongly author-concentrated under the current field and encounter conditions, so a field-wide claim should be restricted and another comparison becomes necessary.

**Use-Status** records what a trace, relation, map, or metaphor may currently do—for example descriptive, heuristic, documentary, artistic, technical, scholarly, residual, or blocked. **Claim Permission** records how far a proposition may travel: preserve only, describe locally, describe distribution under declared conditions, argue cautiously, argue, or block (Zare 2026a).

These fields separate generative usefulness from evidential warrant.

# Part III — Close and distant reading as a pressure loop

## 17. The two-direction rule

The apparatus treats close and distant reading as different scales of encounter that pressure each other's reductions.

Two rules govern the loop:

1. **No distant indication becomes a research relation without source return.** A count, association, cluster, bridge, or anomaly must be inspectable through the exact textual units that produced it.
2. **No local close-reading generalization becomes a corpus-level claim without a field test when the claim concerns distribution, recurrence, typicality, change, or absence.** A compelling verse may generate a hypothesis; the distant apparatus tests where and how far it travels.

This is compatible with the broader hermeneutic use of computer-assisted text analysis described by Rockwell and Sinclair: computational operations become part of interpretive dialogue as part of an interpretive dialogue (Rockwell and Sinclair 2016). It also follows the Mozare method's principle that a new contact surface earns its place when it creates an observation capable of changing the inquiry (Zare 2026a).

## 18. The core loop

A complete cycle proceeds as follows:

1. **State a Research Situation.**
2. **Build Field Charter v0.1.**
3. **Name one or more Seeds.**
4. **Create provisional Object Addresses and Lexical Anchors.**
5. **Run Close Calibration.**
6. **Revise the object boundary or anchors if needed.**
7. **Run deterministic corpus enumeration.**
8. **Construct one or more distant Mapping Objects.**
9. **Mark speculative indications.**
10. **Return to the exact passages that generated the indication.**
11. **Preserve a Trace, revise the indication, or send it to residue.**
12. **Create a Relation-Object only when the relation becomes consequential enough to need persistence.**
13. **Design an Experiment that changes an access condition.**
14. **Generate contrastive Profiles and Mapping Objects.**
15. **Record the difference as a Finding when it changes the inquiry.**
16. **Revise object boundaries, relations, field scope, or claim permission.**
17. **Repeat until the current research obligation can be released with explicit reopening conditions.**

The loop has no privileged direction. A close reading can initiate a distant test; a distant map can initiate a close return; an experiment can expose a new object; a counterexample can reopen the field.

## 19. Outcomes of a distant-to-close return

When the researcher opens the passages behind a distant indication, the system offers five dispositions:

- **retain as trace** — the indication remains consequential but unresolved;
- **narrow** — the relation survives only under more precise conditions;
- **split** — one apparent object or relation becomes several;
- **reject to residue** — the computation created a pattern that close inspection does not support as useful;
- **promote new seed** — the source reveals another object or quality worth following.

The rejected route remains replayable. Failure is part of the research genesis and may later become useful under another field or question.

## 20. Outcomes of a close-to-distant test

A local observation can be tested through the field with corresponding dispositions:

- **distributed** — it recurs across multiple independent corpus regions;
- **concentrated** — it is real within the field but depends heavily on a poet, work, or category;
- **local** — it remains a specific case a specific case with no demonstrated corpus tendency;
- **counterexample-rich** — enough contrary cases appear to pressure the current description;
- **scope-sensitive** — the result changes sharply when the field boundary or encounter scale changes;
- **unresolved** — the available corpus cannot discriminate the issue.

These are descriptions of the current field and operations. None is an ontological verdict.

# Part IV — What the Ganjoor corpus can actually support

## 21. Corpus-native addressables

The current Ganjoor data exposes several stable documentary structures that the apparatus can use directly (Ganjoor 2026; Bashar 2026a):

### Poet

A poet record may include ID, name, nickname, description, Ganjoor URL, birth/death years in Lunar Hijri with validity flags, and birth/death places.

### Category

Category records contain IDs, parent/poet links, title, path, child categories, and ordered poem references. A category can represent a book, formal division, editorial section, or other corpus grouping. Its title must not automatically be reclassified as literary genre.

### Poem

A poem record can include ID, category, title, full title/path, rhyme letters, source name/slug, an optional summary, metre, sections, and verses.

### Section

Sections can carry index, number, section type, verse type, rhyme letters, plain and HTML text, poem format, and couplet count.

### Verse and couplet position

Verses carry order, position, text, couplet index, and section index. This supports exact reconstruction of verse order and couplet-scale encounter units.

### Metre, rhyme, source, and structural format

These are usable as research conditions when they are explicitly present in the source records.


## 22. When a corpus entity becomes an object of inquiry

The corpus offers many addressable entities, but the research method does not assign all of them the same role. The role follows the question.

| Corpus/material entity | Default operational role | When it can become an Object Address | Boundary to preserve |
|---|---|---|---|
| poet | corpus partition, provenance, biographical source | when the poet as person, oeuvre, authorial construction, or mediating entity is itself being followed | poet record ≠ person; oeuvre ≠ biography |
| category/book/collection | structural scope and containment | when editorial organization, book identity, or category behaviour is itself under inquiry | category title ≠ genre automatically |
| poem | textual work address and context unit | when the poem/work is a participant in the research relation | one database record does not settle work/version ontology |
| section | structural/textual unit | when section organization or sectional identity matters to the question | section boundary is corpus/editorial structure unless otherwise evidenced |
| verse/hemistich | smallest source-return unit in many poems | when a verse itself is studied as an object, quotation, version, or relation participant | verse as context unit ≠ object by default |
| couplet | encounter unit reconstructed from verse metadata | when the couplet's composition or identity matters independently | analytical unit ≠ metaphysical entity by declaration |
| metre | formal metadata and comparison condition | when a metre or rhythmic pattern itself is followed across the field | metre ID ≠ total performed rhythm |
| rhyme | formal metadata and comparison condition | when rhyme behaviour is itself the research object | stored rhyme letters remain a corpus representation |
| source name/slug | provenance/filter condition | when edition/source transmission becomes the inquiry | source label ≠ full witness history |
| date/year | empirical coordinate or proxy input | only when a date, calendrical construct, or temporal institution itself becomes consequential | date ≠ Harmanian Time |
| place | empirical coordinate or grouping condition | when place as institution, geography, or poetic/cultural object is under inquiry | birth/death place ≠ poem location |
| neighboring word/token | candidate lexical companion | after repeated or consequential encounters justify a stable Object Address | token ≠ object; co-presence ≠ relation |
| AI summary | derivative interpretive source/Profile | when the act or pattern of summarization itself is under inquiry | summary ≠ poem and ≠ independent evidence |

This role discipline prevents two opposite errors. The system does not deny objecthood to entities because they are metadata, humans, abstractions, or small textual units; OOO supplies no such hierarchy. It also does not force every accessible datum into the research ontology. An entity receives a persistent Object Address when following it as an object changes the inquiry.

## 23. Supported corpus fractions

The Field Builder can construct fields from corpus-native conditions and declared derived conditions.

### 23.1 Native fractions

Researchers can select:

- all published corpus material;
- one poet;
- several poets;
- one category;
- a category subtree;
- selected poem IDs or paths;
- one or several `PoemFormat` values when present;
- metre ID or rhythm;
- rhyme letters;
- source name/slug;
- section type;
- verse type;
- couplet-count range;
- lexical inclusion/exclusion conditions.

Native filters may be combined through intersection, union, or difference.

### 23.2 Derived fractions

Derived fractions are permitted when their construction is stored as part of the field:

- poet-life chronological proxy;
- place-based poet grouping;
- researcher-defined genre grouping assembled from explicit category/format mappings;
- custom author school or movement grouping supplied by the researcher;
- lexical object corpus;
- comparison strata constructed to balance corpus size or other variables.

The interface labels these as **derived** and shows the rule used. A historical period is never implied to be a poem-composition date unless the source contains that evidence.

### 23.3 Field algebra

Researchers should be able to say:

- “Hafez ghazals”;
- “Hafez and Saadi, ghazals only”;
- “all poems in poets whose documented life overlaps 700–800 AH”;
- “that field minus Hafez”;
- “all poems containing one of the approved mirror anchors”;
- “compare Field A with the same field after removing the dominant poet.”

The resulting Scope Specification is machine-readable and preserved in every release.

## 24. Encounter scales

The apparatus supports several declared proximity conditions. They answer different questions and must not be blended silently.

- **verse/hemistich scale** — objects appear within the same verse line record;
- **couplet scale** — objects occur in either half of the same couplet;
- **section scale** — objects occur within the same Ganjoor section;
- **poem scale** — objects occur somewhere in the same poem;
- **category scale** — objects occur within the same selected category/subtree;
- **poet-work scale** — objects occur within the selected oeuvre of one poet;
- **field scale** — objects occur somewhere in the declared Object-Field;
- **token-window scale** — normalized lexical anchors occur within ±N tokens; this is a derived condition whose tokenization and normalization must be recorded.

These scales are not interchangeable notions of relation. Each operation also declares **unit eligibility**: which poem/section/verse records can enter the denominator and why. Prose-like, incomplete, malformed, or structurally exceptional records are not silently forced into couplet logic. The **Scale Survival** operation exists specifically to show how an indication changes when the proximity condition changes.

## 25. QMD/MCP and deterministic enumeration have different responsibilities

The current fork documents four MCP tools—`query`, `get`, `multi_get`, and `status`—and three main collections. `ganjoor` holds full Persian poems for lexical/BM25 retrieval; `ganjoor-fa` and `ganjoor-en` hold embedded Persian and English summaries for semantic retrieval. Summary hits carry a pointer back to the full poem, and the skill explicitly instructs agents to answer from the full poem rather than from the summary snippet alone (Bashar 2026b).

These are retrieval capabilities. Ranked retrieval answers which documents a search system surfaces under a query; it does not prove that every eligible match in a declared field has been enumerated. The upstream static Ganjoor repository itself does not provide a search endpoint.

Ontograph therefore keeps two channels:

- **deterministic corpus channel** — one pinned Ganjoor snapshot or its rebuildable local index; responsible for field membership, Anchor Hit census, eligible-unit construction, reproducible metrics, and source addresses;
- **retrieval/agent channel** — QMD/MCP and AI; responsible for discovery, semantic proposals, contextual source access, plural description, and research assistance.

Every workspace stores the source commit/build identity of both channels. Their synchronization state is `matched`, `known-stale`, or `unknown`. A mixed-snapshot result cannot be exported as a scholarly result without making that mismatch explicit.

The fork itself exposes two distinct surfaces that must not be confused when naming Ontograph's own commands: a `qmd` **CLI verb set** (`qmd search`, `qmd query`, `qmd embed`) used to build and probe the local index directly, and a narrower **MCP tool surface** (`query`, `get`, `multi_get`, `status` only — "there is NO `search` tool," per the fork's own skill) exposed to an agent. Ontograph's CLI (§62) is unrelated to either surface and should not reuse `search` or `query` as verb names for its own deterministic operations (`census`, `map`, `compare`, `ablate` are already distinct enough) — a future implementer skimming both projects should not have to guess which `query` is meant.

# Part V — Distant Ontography

## 26. What Distant Ontography means

**Distant Ontography** is a project-local name for computational arrangements built over a declared Object-Field in order to expose recurrence, distribution, proximity, asymmetry, difference, mediation, or structural dependence that may deserve another research encounter. It is inspired by Bogost's ontographic emphasis on arrangement and repleteness, but corpus statistics are not part of Bogost's philosophical method. The transfer is therefore explicit and limited.

Moretti's account of operationalization provides a useful neighboring precedent: a literary concept becomes researchable through explicit operations whose consequences can be inspected and revised (Moretti 2013). Ganjoor Ontograph adds Profile discipline to that move. An operationalization is recorded as one conditioned access route among others; disagreement between operations can become a Trace instead of being averaged into a synthetic truth.

A distant operation produces a **Mapping Object**. A Mapping Object is a versioned, inspectable arrangement whose source field, units, parameters, transformations, and recovery routes travel with it. It can generate **Speculative Indications**. Those indications organize attention; they do not authorize interpretation by themselves.

Drucker's distinction between data as given and capta as taken is important here. Every map is constituted through selection, segmentation, normalization, counting, weighting, and display. The system therefore exposes those operations instead of presenting its outputs as observer-independent pictures of “what the corpus is” (Drucker 2011; 2014).

## 27. Core operation pack A — Trace an Object

This pack answers two different questions and never merges them:

1. **Lexical census:** where do approved anchors match?
2. **Object-incidence analysis:** where do assessed/qualified occurrences of the addressed research object appear?

### 27.1 Exact anchor-hit census

For each approved lexical anchor, enumerate every matched source location in the pinned field. Store original text, normalized match form, source address, match span, and the anchor/matcher responsible for the hit.

Output: `Anchor Hit Ledger`.

The census is exhaustive **for the declared anchor and matching rules**. It is not called an exhaustive census of the philosophical/research object.

### 27.2 Occurrence assessment layer

Object-level incidence follows the mode declared in §8.1.

- `anchor` reports lexical incidence only.
- `assessed-full` counts only accepted assessments after all eligible hits have been reviewed.
- `assessed-rule` applies a versioned deterministic rule that has passed the study's calibration test.
- `estimated` reports a weighted estimate from a declared probability sample together with uncertainty; it never produces an “exact object count.”

v2.2.0 required that an `estimated`-mode result carry "sampling frame, strata, random seed, weights/estimator, ambiguity treatment, and uncertainty interval" but never named a default estimator or interval — without one, the scalability gate in §70 has no implementable route in v0.1, since "probability-sample estimate" would mean something different in every study. The provisional default (Appendix C.3; replaceable after implementation evidence, not a methodological invariant): stratify eligible units by poet (or by the study's declared strata), draw a simple random sample within each stratum, and estimate object-incidence prevalence as the stratified proportion estimator with a **Wilson score interval** (or, when the sampling fraction exceeds 10% of a stratum, the same estimator with a finite-population correction). The estimator identity and its exact parameters still travel in `OccurrencePolicy.estimator` and `.uncertainty_method`; this default only ensures v0.1 has something concrete to implement and test against rather than an unbounded design space.

Every result card names the mode. `ambiguous` hits remain available for inspection and are excluded from exact binary object incidence unless the study defines and justifies another policy.

In v0.1, **object-level pairwise co-incidence and relation matrices require `assessed-full` or `assessed-rule` for all participating Object Addresses**. Estimated single-object incidence cannot be silently combined into an exact pair matrix. A later release may add a validated joint-estimation procedure.

### 27.3 Unit incidence

For a chosen eligible unit `u` and object address `o`:

`I(u,o) = 1` only when at least one hit in `u` satisfies an exact object-incidence policy (`assessed-full` or `assessed-rule`); otherwise `0`. Anchor-mode matrices are named separately, and estimated mode uses an estimator rather than this exact binary census.

Eligibility and denominator rules are stored with the operation. This distinguishes anchor-hit count, assessed occurrence count, and the number of textual units in which the addressed object is treated as present.

### 27.4 Prevalence

For field `F` partitioned into eligible units `U_F`:

`Prevalence(o | F) = units containing o / total eligible units`.

The denominator is always displayed.

### 27.5 Spread

Show the number and share of distinct poems, categories, and poets containing the object. Spread is descriptive and should not be conflated with dispersion.

### 27.6 Concentration

Show top-source shares: how much of the object's incidence is supplied by its top poet, top category, and top poem where meaningful. A high total count accompanied by extreme concentration is shown as such.

### 27.7 Dispersion

For comparisons across partitions of unequal size, the implementation may offer a **named, versioned dispersion measure** from Gries's deviation-of-proportions family. “Dispersion” is not a free-floating metric label: the exact formula/variant, partition definition, token/unit basis, and software implementation are part of `OperationSpec`. The interface never shows a dispersion value without raw counts and partition sizes (Gries 2008; 2024).

Frequency and dispersion remain separate dimensions because frequency alone can misrepresent how widely a form is distributed (Gries 2008; 2024).

### Researcher-facing result

The researcher sees a sentence such as:

> `آینه` occurs in 214 poems across 38 poets in this field. Forty-six percent of poem-level incidence comes from the top three poets. Distribution is therefore recurrent but concentrated. Open the dominant poets, compare with the remainder, or create an ablation experiment.

The wording describes the current field; it does not infer literary significance.

## 28. Core operation pack B — What meets this object?

This pack searches for recurrent companions without treating proximity as a settled relation.

### 28.1 Typed co-incidence matrix

For eligible units × object addresses, construct binary matrix `A` from the operation's declared occurrence policy, **not directly from raw lexical matches**. Then:

`C = Aᵀ A`

where off-diagonal `C[i,j]` is the number of eligible units in which the currently assessed addresses `i` and `j` co-occur under the declared encounter scale. The diagonal records unit incidence. A separate `A_anchor` matrix may be generated for purely lexical exploration and must be labelled as such.

This generalizes the first Black Bird annex, where Research Note Object × Field Object incidence was used to expose recurrence and co-incidence without collapsing the research field into a single graph (Zare 2026d).

### 28.2 Conditional association

For two object addresses A and B:

`P(B | A) = co-incidence(A,B) / incidence(A)`

and separately:

`P(A | B) = co-incidence(A,B) / incidence(B)`.

The difference can expose asymmetry in textual distribution. It is called **conditional association**, not causation or direction of influence.

### 28.3 Lift

Optional lift compares observed co-incidence with what would be expected from independent prevalence:

`Lift(A,B) = P(A∩B) / [P(A)P(B)]`.

Because lift can become unstable for rare objects, the interface always shows support count, field size, and a configurable minimum-support guard. PMI and other rare-event-sensitive association measures are deferred from the default interface.

The first release does **not** describe lift as statistical significance. If companions are ranked as “unusually associated,” the operation must add a declared reference condition (for example a permutation/shuffle that preserves the relevant field partition) or avoid the word “unusual.” This moves the simplest null/reference condition from the experimental reserve into the core validity guard.

### 28.4 Neighbor discovery

When the researcher has one seed object, the system can propose high-support neighboring lexical candidates from declared windows. These are **candidate anchors**, not automatically Object Addresses. The researcher may inspect, accept, reject, or promote them.

## 29. Core operation pack C — Relation Scale Profile

A relation that survives only at a broad scale differs from one that repeatedly occurs in tight contact. The apparatus recomputes co-incidence across an ordered scale ladder.

For broad scale `b` and narrower scale `n`:

`ScaleSurvival(A,B; b→n) = C_n(A,B) / C_b(A,B)` when `C_b > 0`.

The display always includes raw counts at each scale.

Example:

| Scale | `mirror–rust` shared units |
|---|---:|
| same poem | 74 |
| same section | 61 |
| same couplet | 39 |
| same verse | 27 |
| ±5 tokens | 14 |

The calculation does not tell the researcher which scale is “real.” It makes the dependence of the apparent relation on encounter scale inspectable. The close-reading return can then sample cases that survive tightly and cases that only exist broadly.

## 30. Core operation pack D — Compare Fields

A researcher can duplicate a Field Charter, change one declared condition, and compare the resulting Profiles.

Examples include:

- Hafez vs. Saadi;
- ghazal vs. masnavi within a compatible field;
- a poet-life period proxy A vs. proxy B;
- one source grouping vs. another;
- full field vs. field with a dominant poet removed.

The comparison reports:

- raw incidence in both fields;
- prevalence in both fields;
- dispersion/concentration differences;
- companions gained or lost;
- relation-scale changes;
- close-reading cases responsible for the largest difference.

A smoothed log ratio may be offered as an advanced effect-size view, but it is always subordinate to raw support. Rare events are never ranked as strong differences solely because a normalized score is large.

## 31. Core operation pack E — Ablation

Ablation deliberately removes a component and recomputes a declared result. It is useful for testing false centres and dependence.

A researcher can remove:

- one poet;
- one poem or category;
- one lexical anchor variant;
- one object address;
- one Relation-Object from a research-made relation layer;
- one derived field rule.

For metric `M`:

`AblationRetention = M(after removal) / M(before removal)`.

The output names what was removed and what remained.

Example:

> Removing Poet X reduces `mirror–rust` couplet co-incidence from 46 to 8. The relation retains 17.4% of its previous support. This indicates strong dependence on Poet X within the current field; it does not explain that dependence.

Ablation is also used to pressure network hubs before treating them as meaningful centres.

## 32. Research-made mediation after Relation-Objects exist

Once the research has created a relation layer, the apparatus can compare textual recurrence with researcher-made mediation.

Let matrix `B` have object addresses as rows and Relation-Objects as columns, with `B[o,r]=1` when object `o` participates in relation `r`.

Then:

- **Mediational Incidence:** `MI(o) = Σ_r B[o,r]`;
- **Relation-mediated thickness:** `T_R = B Bᵀ`.

These operations derive from the Black Bird Research Annex, where Black Bird and Corpse had equal recurrence across source-bearing research notes but different incidence in the made Relation-Object layer. The calculation revealed that the entry-object and the densest relation-body were not identical; the value lay in the discrepancy and the return it prompted, not in declaring a universal importance score (Zare 2026d).

### 32.1 Corpus/research mediation comparison

The Ganjoor apparatus does not compress textual recurrence and research-made mediation into one score. It displays a two-dimensional diagnostic:

- high textual recurrence / high research mediation;
- high textual recurrence / low research mediation;
- low textual recurrence / high research mediation;
- low textual recurrence / low research mediation.

This lets researchers ask where their own inquiry has thickened a rare relation or ignored a recurrent corridor. It is a reflexive audit of research construction, not an automated diagnosis of researcher bias.

## 33. Multiplex graph diagnostics

Graph analysis is allowed only after the relation layer is named. At minimum, the apparatus keeps the following networks separate:

1. **corpus containment layer** — poet/category/poem/section containment;
2. **textual encounter layer** — object addresses co-present under a declared unit;
3. **research relation layer** — Relation-Objects created by the inquiry;
4. **evidence/provenance layer** — sources, Mapping Objects, Profiles, and claims;
5. **navigation layer** — reader traversal or interface route when recorded.

Centrality in one layer is never called “importance” in the project as a whole. Betweenness, articulation points, connected components, or community diagnostics may be used as **bridge diagnostics** inside a named projection, followed by ablation and close return. These remain advanced operations, hidden from the default interface until the field is large enough to need them.

# Part VI — Close-reading capabilities

## 34. Source Return is mandatory

Every computational result must be reversible to the source units that produced it. A heatmap cell, pair count, dispersion score, field difference, or network bridge opens an evidence drawer containing:

- exact verse text;
- coupled verse when relevant;
- section and poem context;
- poet and category;
- corpus snapshot/path;
- lexical anchor(s) that produced the match;
- normalization/transformation applied;
- encounter scale;
- Mapping Object and operation parameters.

A result that cannot return to its contributing corpus units is not eligible for scholarly use.

## 35. Context ladder

The Close Reader lets the researcher change context without rerunning the query:

**match → verse → couplet → section → poem → category → poet corpus → Field**.

The purpose is not to imply that wider context is always better. Each scale makes other relations available and hides others. The researcher can save any scale as a Profile of the addressed object or relation.

## 36. Concordance and stratified reading sets

The system can produce a KWIC/concordance view for lexical anchors, but it also creates **stratified reading sets** chosen from the distant Profile:

- frequent/dominant contexts;
- rare contexts;
- different poets;
- different categories or formats;
- tight vs. broad co-incidence cases;
- counterexamples;
- cases responsible for field differences;
- cases removed by ablation.

This keeps close reading attached to the structure of the field instead of becoming a hand-picked illustration of the researcher's preferred result.

## 37. Plural description

When a passage becomes a Trace, the Close Reader asks for more than one possible description before a relation type is stabilized. Human and AI collaborators may propose alternatives, but each description is attributed.

For an encounter between mirror and rust, candidate descriptions could include:

- literal material degradation inside the poetic scene;
- a comparison in which physical degradation supplies qualities for another object;
- an inherited poetic collocation;
- a local lexical adjacency with no wider relation;
- a relation whose material and symbolic operations cannot yet be separated.

The apparatus does not require all descriptions to survive. Their early coexistence prevents the first fluent interpretation from silently becoming the relation's ontology.

## 38. Object-boundary work

Close reading may show that one Object Address is doing too much work. The researcher can **split** it while retaining genealogy.

For example, an initial `MIRROR` address may later require distinct addresses for:

- a physical reflective surface explicitly present in the scene;
- mirror as an object in a simile/metaphor relation;
- a named textual/conceptual construction that repeatedly uses mirror language but no longer describes material interaction.

The project does not prescribe these three classes in advance. They are examples of a split that must arise from the material. The reverse operation, **merge**, is also possible when two addresses prove operationally redundant. Both operations preserve aliases and prior mappings.

## 39. Counterexample and negative-case tray

The researcher can pin passages that weaken or complicate a relation description. Counterexamples remain attached to the Relation-Object. The tray distinguishes:

- counter-evidence against a proposition;
- negative case that narrows scope;
- absence under one declared condition;
- ambiguous example;
- extraction or normalization error.

This prevents “examples that did not fit” from disappearing during synthesis.

## 40. Summary contrast as a Profile comparison

Ganjoor poem summaries, including AI-generated summaries where present, can be compared with the poem text as a separate research operation. The summary is treated as a derivative interpretive Profile. The system can ask:

Two summary sources exist in the current substrate and must not be conflated: `PoemSummary`, produced by Ganjoor itself and present directly in the upstream JSON, and the fork's separately generated English semantic summary, produced by "any OpenAI-compatible API — pluggable" per the fork's own documentation. The Persian summary is a fixed field of a pinned corpus snapshot; the English summary is not — its wording depends on which model, prompt, and pipeline version generated it, and the fork's README treats that pipeline as swappable across rebuilds. A Profile built from an AI-generated summary must therefore record the summarizer model identity, version, and prompt/pipeline version (§52), not only the corpus snapshot, or two Profiles claiming to be "the English summary Profile" for the same poem could silently disagree.

- which objects/relations present in the source are retained?
- which material operations disappear?
- which human, mystical, ethical, or thematic descriptions are introduced?
- which ambiguities are collapsed?

The project does **not** presuppose that the summaries are anthropocentric. That proposition becomes testable only after a declared sample and comparison procedure are run.

# Part VII — The researcher experience

## 41. Primary interface: guided research conversation

The first release is designed for researchers with little or no technical background. The **research engine is interface-independent**. Its normative core is a Python library plus deterministic CLI/JSON contracts. An agent skill can be the primary interface in runtimes that can actually invoke the engine (for example a shell-capable agent harness). A skill file alone does not create execution capability.

Where the host cannot execute the CLI, Ontograph requires a thin tool adapter (MCP or equivalent) exposing the same engine contracts. The existing Persian-poetry MCP is a retrieval server and is not silently redefined as the Ontograph analysis engine.

The agent does not ask researchers to choose “DP,” “lift,” “AᵀA,” or “betweenness centrality” unless they open the method details. It asks questions such as:

- What object or objects do you want to follow?
- Which part of Ganjoor should count as the field?
- Should two objects count as meeting in the same couplet, the same poem, or another scale?
- Do you want to see where this object is widespread or where it is concentrated?
- Do you want to compare this field with another one?
- Should we remove the dominant poet and see what survives?
- Open the passages behind this pattern?
- Keep this as a trace, revise it, or discard it?

Every conversational action produces a durable record in the research workspace.

## 42. Research surfaces

The following are **interaction responsibilities**, not a claim that a custom GUI already exists. In v0.1 they may be rendered conversationally; a later browser UI may give them persistent visual surfaces.

### 42.1 Field Builder

Purpose: define and preview the Object-Field.

Shows:

- pinned corpus version;
- included poets/categories/poems;
- native vs. derived filters;
- resulting poem/verse counts;
- blind zones and proxy warnings;
- field comparison or ablation options.

### 42.2 Seed and Object Workbench

Purpose: maintain Seeds, Object Addresses, lexical anchors, aliases, and split/merge history.

### 42.3 Close Calibration

Purpose: inspect representative and ambiguous source examples before scaling.

### 42.4 Distant Workbench

Purpose: run prebuilt research operations and show Mapping Objects with source-return actions.

Default modes:

- Trace an Object;
- What Meets It?;
- Test a Relation;
- Compare Fields;
- Remove and Recalculate;
- Audit Our Research, once Relation-Objects exist.

### 42.5 Close Reader

Purpose: inspect exact source material through the context ladder, annotate traces, compare Profiles, and preserve counterexamples.

### 42.6 Trace Tray

Purpose: hold low-cost consequential indications before relation formalization.

### 42.7 Relation Workbench

Purpose: develop Relation-Objects with participants, descriptions, evidence, counter-evidence, Profiles, Mapping Objects, experiments, and permissions.

### 42.8 Experiment Builder

Purpose: state what condition should change and why, then compare pre/post Profiles without hiding the transformation.

### 42.9 Profile Comparator

Purpose: place two or more access records beside one another and make differences explicit.

### 42.10 Research Release

Purpose: generate a human-readable and machine-replayable state of the current inquiry.

## 43. Progressive disclosure

The default view shows the research consequence before the formula. A result card contains:

1. plain-language observation;
2. raw counts and denominators;
3. source-return button;
4. action choices;
5. “How was this made?” disclosure containing the operation, formula, parameters, reductions, and limitations.

The system therefore remains usable without technical knowledge while retaining enough detail for reconstruction and critique.

## 44. No graph-first design

The interface does not open with a network. A graph is generated only when a declared relation projection benefits from spatial inspection. The graph edge opens the underlying Mapping Object or Relation-Object. Filtering, layout, and projection rules are visible in the graph's method drawer.

This decision follows both the Black Bird precedent—where Relation-Objects are first-class nodes and visible edges are renderings of richer participant structures—and Drucker's warning that graphical conventions can naturalize constructed relations (Zare 2026e; Drucker 2011).

# Worked example

## 45. Mirror as a seed: one complete illustrative cycle

The sequence below demonstrates the apparatus without asserting corpus results that have not been calculated.

A researcher begins from a material suspicion: in several remembered passages, mirror seems to appear through surface operations such as polishing, obscuration, reflection, and degradation. `آینه` enters first as a Seed, not as an ontology. The Research Situation asks: **Which material encounters around mirror recur beyond isolated passages, and which remain local to particular poets or forms?**

The study creates a provisional `MIRROR` Object Address and approves `آینه` and `آیینه` as initial Lexical Anchors. The apparatus records how those forms are normalized, then enumerates Anchor Hits inside a small calibration frame. A stratified sample is opened through the context ladder. Some hits may involve a physical reflective surface; others may enter comparison or figurative construction; some may remain ambiguous. Their Occurrence Assessments become evidence about whether the current address is usable, whether the anchors need narrowing, or whether `MIRROR` should split.

The researcher then constructs a Field Charter for a selected group of poets. Deterministic enumeration produces the complete **Anchor Hit Ledger** for the approved anchor rules. If the study chooses `assessed-full`, every hit is assessed before an exact object-level incidence claim is made. If the hit set is too large, the study can choose `estimated` mode and report an estimated mirror incidence with its sampling design and uncertainty; it cannot call that estimate a complete object census.

Suppose the study reaches an exact assessed mode and finds a recurrent lexical/object companion such as `زنگار`. A couplet-scale Mapping Object marks the pair as a **Speculative Indication** and offers three immediate actions: inspect shared couplets, compare with broader poem-scale contact, or preserve the indication as a Trace. The researcher opens the passages first.

The close return may divide what the matrix had placed together. Some `mirror–rust` passages may stage material surface degradation; others may use that operation in a comparison involving heart, face, perception, or purification. The researcher records more than one plausible description and preserves the contact as a Trace. It is not yet typed as `symbolizes`, `causes`, or `represents`.

The next Experiment asks two discriminating questions: **Does the apparent relation survive when the encounter scale tightens, and does it survive the removal of the dominant poet?** The baseline Profile uses poem-scale co-incidence. The engine constructs couplet-, verse-, and ±5-token Mapping Objects, then performs an ablation. Every map retains its eligible units, denominator, assessment mode, normalization/tokenizer, software version, and source-return manifest.

If the relation survives tightly but collapses after removing one poet, the resulting Finding narrows the claim. The relation may deserve a persistent Relation-Object inside this study, while a field-wide statement remains blocked because the support is strongly author-concentrated.

Close reading can then expose a new consequential entity—polishing cloth, dust, a body part, a tool, or another material. The researcher may promote it as a new Seed and begin another loop without erasing the route that produced it.

A Research Release preserves the pinned corpus and retrieval snapshots, Field Charter, Object Addresses, anchors, calibration receipt, Anchor Hit Ledger, assessment policy, load-bearing Profiles, Mapping Objects, source returns, Traces, Experiments, Findings, Relation-Objects, permissions, event history, residue, and reopening conditions. The release does not claim what mirror finally *is*. It states what this inquiry established, through which operations, and where another encounter can still change the result.

# Part IX — Technical architecture

## 56. Source layer

The pinned Ganjoor repository remains the documentary source of truth. Upstream JSON is never modified by Ontograph research operations.

Each workspace records:

- canonical Ganjoor repository and exact commit SHA;
- manifest SHA-256 and `GeneratedAtUtc`;
- schema version;
- poet/poem counts;
- fork commit SHA when fork-generated Markdown/QMD is used;
- generated Markdown manifest/hash when used;
- QMD collection/index build hash and source commit;
- a **synchronization verdict**: `matched`, `known-stale`, or `unknown`.

A study cannot combine deterministic results from one corpus commit with retrieval results from another without displaying that mismatch.

**Licensing chain.** Per the upstream fork's own `NOTICE.md` (verified 2026-08-16, re-audited 2026-08-27): the classical poem texts are public domain; `ganjoor-data`'s compilation, structure, and Ganjoor's own Persian AI summaries carry no declared open license and are used here under the same attribution convention Ganjoor's own ecosystem follows; any fork-generated English summary is separately MIT-licensed by the fork. Ontograph does not alter this chain, but every workspace must record it once at intake (`CorpusSnapshot.license_notice`) and every `ResearchRelease` must carry it forward verbatim (§55) rather than re-deriving or forgetting it at export time. A public research release that strips this attribution is out of policy regardless of its scholarly merit.

## 57. Derived research index

Exhaustive corpus operations should not repeatedly scan several gigabytes of source files. The Ontograph layer builds a **rebuildable local SQLite research index** from the pinned JSON/Markdown corpus. It is derived infrastructure and is not treated as source evidence.

Suggested tables:

- `poets`
- `categories`
- `poems`
- `sections`
- `verses`
- `couplets`
- `metres`
- `sources`
- `normalized_verses`
- `token_offsets`

Indexes cover poet/category/poem IDs, field-relevant metadata, normalized text, and path references. The builder emits a manifest linking every derived row to upstream source addresses.

SQLite is preferred for v0.1 because it is local, inspectable, transaction-safe, available through Python's standard library, and sufficient for the expected corpus size. A graph database is not required to represent the research method.

## 58. Persian normalization

Normalization is versioned and deliberately conservative. The default pipeline may normalize:

- Arabic/Persian Yeh variants;
- Arabic/Persian Kaf variants;
- Unicode normalization form;
- zero-width non-joiner policy;
- optional diacritic removal for lexical matching;
- whitespace.

Punctuation deletion, stemming, lemmatization, compound splitting, synonym expansion, and semantic expansion are **not** default because they can alter poetic and lexical distinctions. They may be introduced as named transformations when a research situation requires them.

The implementation must also define Persian token boundaries explicitly for token-window operations: ZWNJ/half-space behavior, punctuation boundaries, Arabic/Persian letter variants, combining marks/diacritics, and clitic attachment. A token window is invalid for release if its tokenizer/version is missing.

Every match retains original text and offsets so normalization remains reversible at the reading layer.

## 59. Analysis engine

A Python module performs deterministic field construction and calculations.

The engine exposes stable JSON input/output contracts so the conversational skill, CLI, tests, MCP adapter, and a later GUI can use the same operations. The repository pins the Python/runtime dependencies and records the engine version in every `OperationSpec`; reproducibility cannot depend on an unrecorded local environment.

**Workspace version control.** v2.2.0 specified an append-only `EventRecord` stream (§51) and JSONL/YAML workspace files but left open whether the workspace itself is under version control — a gap for a project whose central discipline is exactly "results must be reversible to what produced them." Each `ontograph-workspaces/<study-id>/` directory SHOULD itself be a git repository; each `ontograph release` invocation (§62) SHOULD produce a corresponding tagged commit. This is not a new mechanism — it reuses the same commit-pin discipline the corpus and fork layers already impose on themselves (§56) and applies it reflexively to the research layer, giving free diffing, rollback, and reviewable multi-collaborator merges without inventing a bespoke concurrency model. A single-writer-per-branch convention is sufficient for v0.1; concurrent multi-researcher editing of one live workspace remains out of scope (§74) until a study actually requires it.

The skill's job is orchestration, not interpretation-by-default. AI outputs enter as attributed proposals. An agent may propose anchor variants, candidate descriptions, traces, field expansions, or experiments; it cannot silently promote a proposal into evidence or a Relation-Object into a scholarly claim.

## 62. CLI as reproducibility layer

Example commands:

```bash

ontograph field build mirror-study --poet hafez --category ghazal
```

The conversational skill should generate these operations internally; researchers do not need to type them.

## 63. Tool adapter contract

The engine must be callable from the target runtime. For shell-capable harnesses, the deterministic CLI is sufficient. For hosts that cannot execute the CLI directly, a thin MCP (or equivalent) adapter is required in v0.1. The existing QMD server supplies retrieval/source fetch only; it does not remove the need for an execution adapter for Ontograph calculations and persistent study mutation.

A minimal adapter may expose:

- `field_create`
- `field_compare`
- `object_trace`
- `relation_test`
- `profile_get`
- `experiment_run`
- `relation_update`
- `release_get`

The MCP schema must remain a technical interface to the research method, not a new philosophical ontology.

If the adapter is implemented as MCP, it must speak the same protocol version the existing retrieval server already uses (2025-11-25 at audit time): capture the `mcp-session-id` header returned from `initialize`, resend it as `Mcp-Session-Id` on every subsequent request, and send `notifications/initialized` before issuing tool calls. This is not an Ontograph-specific design choice — it is what the persian-poetry MCP server's own operating skill already documents as required (Bashar 2026b) — but v2.2.0 did not carry the requirement into its own adapter contract, which would have made a literal implementation of §63 fail against the very MCP client conventions the rest of the project depends on. §77 below settles, for the Claude Code target specifically, that this MCP path is not actually needed there.

# Part X — Design traceability

## 64. Bridge contracts

The table below makes the principal transfers explicit.

| Philosophical/research pressure | Methodological operation | Design consequence | Technical consequence | Non-transfer / failure guard |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Object exceeds current access (Harman) | contrastive Profiles; reopening | never show one representation as complete | versioned `ProfileRecord`; no destructive overwrite | Profile ≠ object; missing data ≠ withdrawal |
| Ontography exposes heterogeneous units before explanatory closure (Bogost) | inventory; Object-Field | Field Builder precedes graph/explanation | typed address registry; flexible scope | inventory does not establish relation |
| Metaphorical access is indirect (Harman/Bogost) | Disciplined Metaphoring | show source operation, transfer, remainder | `BridgeRecord`; provenance of transformation | operational resemblance ≠ identity/influence |
| Carpentry makes through construction (Bogost) | experiment/build to learn | experiment can create a new contact surface | event log; `ExperimentRecord`; `FindingRecord` | artifact existence ≠ finding |
| Humanistic display constructs capta (Drucker) | Mapping Object + Reduction Record | every visualization has “How made?” and source return | `OperationSpec`, `ReductionRecord`, source-return manifest | map ≠ ontology; coordinate system is constructed |
| Frequency can conceal concentration (Gries) | separate recurrence, spread, dispersion | result cards show counts + distribution | tuple metrics, no universal importance score | high count ≠ widespreadness |
| Close/distant methods expose different scales | two-direction pressure loop | every distant result opens close reader; local claims can launch field tests | shared IDs between mapping and source records | neither scale automatically outranks the other |
| Relation meaning may remain unstable | Trace → Relation-Object lifecycle | low-cost trace tray; relation editor later | separate trace/relation stores | co-occurrence ≠ relation; relation record ≠ proof |
| Claims require different permissions | Use-Status + Claim Permission | system distinguishes heuristic, artistic, scholarly use | permission fields and export gates | generative value ≠ evidential warrant |
| Lexical retrieval does not settle object presence | Anchor Hit → Occurrence Assessment | results name lexical vs object-incidence mode | `AnchorHitRecord`, `OccurrenceAssessment`, `OccurrencePolicy` | anchor match ≠ object occurrence |
| Retrieval and source corpora can drift | snapshot synchronization contract | mismatch is visible before calculation/export | commit/build hashes + `matched`/`known-stale`/`unknown` verdict | current ranking layer ≠ current upstream corpus |
| Conversational intent needs executable operations | interface-independent engine | conversation orchestrates rather than simulates analysis | Python/JSON core + CLI or tool adapter | skill instructions ≠ execution capability |

A component that cannot be justified through such a route should not enter the first implementation.

# Part XI — Validation and pressure tests

## 65. Deterministic corpus tests

The engine must test:

- corpus snapshot and manifest hashes;
- source-file count against manifest;
- field membership reproducibility;
- category subtree expansion;
- poem/section/verse/couplet reconstruction;
- anchor normalization round-trip to original text;
- tokenizer fixtures for ZWNJ, diacritics, Arabic/Persian Yeh/Kaf, punctuation, and mixed-script spans;
- Anchor Hit counts against direct source scans for fixture corpora;
- Occurrence Assessment policy tests proving rejected/ambiguous hits do not silently enter exact object-incidence;
- `estimated`-mode fixtures verifying sampling weights/estimator/uncertainty and prohibiting exact-count wording;
- pairwise object-level mappings blocked when any participant is only in unsupported estimated mode;
- formula correctness for incidence, co-incidence, prevalence, conditional association, lift, scale survival, ablation, and mediation;
- source-return completeness for every Mapping Object;
- EventRecord append-only integrity and replay of state-changing actions;
- reproducible calibration sampling from stored random seed/strata;
- corpus/QMD synchronization guard: mismatched source commits must be visible and block mixed-snapshot scholarly export;
- operation denominator/eligibility reconstruction;
- engine/CLI/adapter contract equivalence on shared fixtures.

## 66. Epistemic contract tests

Scenario tests verify that the system does **not**:

- treat lexical anchor **or raw Anchor Hit** as settled object occurrence automatically;
- promote candidate companion to Object Address without a decision;
- label co-incidence as causation/influence;
- call a chronological proxy a poem date;
- call category title a genre unless the mapping is declared;
- convert zero count into ontological absence;
- merge textual and Relation-Object networks into one centrality score;
- create a scholarly claim directly from an AI proposal;
- hide normalization or field reduction;
- produce a metric without source return;
- present an estimate as a complete census;
- combine a stale retrieval snapshot with a newer deterministic corpus without a visible synchronization warning;
- overwrite earlier Profiles when conditions change;
- delete rejected traces from research history.

## 67. Close–distant loop tests

Use crafted miniature corpora where the true distribution is known.

### Test A — concentrated frequency

Object A appears 100 times, 90 in one poet. The interface must not describe it simply as broadly recurrent.

### Test B — scale collapse

A and B occur in the same poems repeatedly but almost never in the same couplets. The Relation Scale Profile must make the collapse visible and source-return both classes of examples.

### Test C — lexical ambiguity

One anchor matches two distinct uses. Close Calibration must allow the researcher to split the Object Address or narrow the anchor before a field-wide interpretation.

### Test D — ablation false centre

A relation appears strong because one poet supplies most instances. Removing that poet must expose the dependence.

### Test E — local close-reading insight

A researcher identifies a material relation in one poem. The system must be able to test its recurrence without rewriting the local reading if the relation remains local.

### Test F — research-made mediation

A rare textual relation receives many Relation-Objects. The system must show the difference between corpus recurrence and research attention without calling either one truth or bias.

## 68. User testing with nontechnical researchers

A pilot succeeds only if a researcher with no command-line knowledge can:

1. create a field;
2. define a seed and anchors;
3. understand a calibration sample;
4. run a recurrence/dispersion view;
5. inspect a companion relation;
6. open exact poems behind a calculation;
7. save a trace;
8. vary one condition;
9. compare the result;
10. export a Research Release.

The researcher should be able to explain, in their own words, why a displayed pattern is provisional and how to return to the material that produced it. If the interface teaches confidence in the score instead of reconstructability, the design has failed.

# Part XII — Scope, implementation sequence, and reserves

## 69. Implementation gates before claiming the apparatus works

The project is **not validated by this specification alone**. The following gates separate design validity from working software:

1. **Corpus pin gate** — exact Ganjoor commit + manifest hash are recorded; any fork/QMD snapshot mismatch is known.
2. **Lexical/object gate** — Anchor Hits and object Occurrence Assessments are separate in schema, metrics, and UI language.
3. **Deterministic-engine gate** — field construction, census, scale, comparison, ablation, and source-return pass fixture tests.
4. **Runtime gate** — the chosen agent interface can actually invoke those operations through CLI or a tool adapter.
5. **Research-loop gate** — at least one end-to-end study is replayed from Field Charter to Research Release and independently reconstructed from its release package.

Until all five pass, the correct status is **implementation specification**, not “working research apparatus.”

## 70. Occurrence-assessment scalability gate

The first release must prove that the lexical/object distinction remains usable at realistic corpus scale. At least one end-to-end study must use an Object Address with enough Anchor Hits to pressure the assessment workflow.

The implementation must demonstrate one of three defensible routes:

1. **complete assessment** — every eligible hit is assessed;
2. **validated rule** — a deterministic rule is calibrated against reviewed material and its limits are recorded;
3. **probability-sample estimate** — object incidence is estimated from a declared sampling design with uncertainty.

The system fails this gate if it falls back to raw lexical matches while labelling them object occurrences, or if an estimated result is presented as an exact census.

## 71. v0.1 — minimum complete research loop

The first implementation should include only the components needed for a full research cycle:

- pinned corpus snapshot and derived SQLite research index;
- Field Builder with native and declared derived filters;
- Seed/Object Address/Lexical Anchor records;
- Close Calibration;
- exact Anchor Hit census plus Occurrence Assessment/OccurrencePolicy layer;
- full, validated-rule, and estimated object-incidence workflows;
- context ladder/source return;
- recurrence, prevalence, spread, concentration;
- simple dispersion view with raw counts;
- typed co-incidence;
- conditional association;
- relation scale profile;
- field comparison;
- ablation;
- Mapping Objects, Operation Specs, and Reduction Records;
- Trace Tray;
- Relation-Object workbench;
- Profiles and Experiments;
- Use-Status and Claim Permission;
- Python core engine with pinned dependencies;
- deterministic CLI;
- conversational skill;
- runtime tool adapter when the target host cannot execute the CLI;
- append-only EventRecord genesis log;
- versioned Research Release;
- deterministic and epistemic tests.

This is enough to demonstrate the contribution without a large custom UI or graph database.

## 72. v0.2 — reflexive relation layer

After actual studies have produced enough Relation-Objects, add:

- Mediational Incidence;
- relation-mediated thickness;
- corpus/research mediation comparison;
- relation-layer ablation;
- multiplex bridge diagnostics;
- richer replay of research genesis;
- optional local browser workbench if conversational output proves insufficient.

## 73. Experimental reserve

The following remain deliberately divergent. They should enter only when a concrete Research Situation can state what new observation they would make possible:

- sequence and order profiles within poems;
- recurrence trajectories across moving poet-life chronological proxies;
- higher-order relations among three or more object addresses;
- matrix decomposition or latent-factor views;
- spectral/community diagnostics;
- intermedial ontographic machines using spatialization, sound, animation, or projection;
- comparison of source text with multiple model-generated interpretive Profiles;
- export of Ganjoor studies into the broader Ontograph workbench;
- dedicated MCP server for persistent multi-agent research.

These possibilities are preserved because they may later create useful encounters. They are not architecture commitments.

## 74. Explicitly outside the first project

The project does not require:

- external manuscript collection;
- corpus-wide manual thematic tagging;
- a universal Persian literary ontology;
- automatic metaphor/symbol classification;
- automatic historical-influence detection;
- corpus-wide LLM labeling;
- a universal Persian lemmatizer before a research problem requires it;
- a graph database;
- a large public dashboard;
- the assumption that Ganjoor summaries are inferior or anthropocentric before testing them.

# Part XIII — Claude Code implementation binding

Parts IX–XII were deliberately written host-agnostic: the engine is a Python/JSON core, the skill is "the primary interface in runtimes that can actually invoke the engine," and Appendix C.3 left "whether a target runtime uses direct CLI execution or an MCP/equivalent adapter" as a provisional, unresolved default. That neutrality is correct for a specification meant to outlive one runtime. It also means the document, as written, cannot be picked up and implemented against a specific agent host without one more binding decision. This part makes that decision for Claude Code, since that is the declared implementation target.

## 75. Claude Code resolves the CLI/adapter question directly

Claude Code is exactly the "shell-capable agent harness" §41 already names as sufficient for direct CLI execution. It can invoke arbitrary Bash commands, parse their JSON stdout, and read/write the workspace filesystem directly. This means:

- the deterministic CLI (§62) is the **only** execution path required for the primary implementation target;
- the MCP/tool-adapter path (§63) is **not required** for Claude Code and should be treated as a compatibility path for a future non-shell host (a hosted chat surface without shell access), not as a parallel v0.1 deliverable;
- this resolves Appendix C.3's "whether a target runtime uses direct CLI execution or an MCP/equivalent adapter" as a settled decision for this deployment specifically, without promoting it to a methodological invariant (§C.1) for the project as a whole — a different host could still need the adapter.

## 77. Division of labor with `persian-poetry-mcp`

The fork's existing skill already owns discovery: `query`/`get`/`multi_get`/`status` against the QMD collections, for finding candidate poems, quoting a remembered line, or answering "what does Ganjoor say about X." The Ontograph skill must not reimplement or shadow that — it owns everything §25 assigns to the deterministic channel: field membership, Anchor Hit census, calibration, mapping construction, comparison, ablation, and release. A concrete rule for the agent: if the researcher's question is "find/quote/discover," route to `persian-poetry-mcp`; if it names or implies a Seed, Object Address, field, calibration, mapping, experiment, or release, route to `persian-poetry-ontograph`. Where a researcher's discovery turn surfaces a candidate lexical companion worth tracking, the Ontograph skill ingests it as a **candidate anchor** (§28.4) — never as an accepted Object Address — exactly as it would from any other proposal source.

## 78. Concrete invocation pattern

`SKILL.md` instructs the agent to shell out to the pinned engine and treat its JSON stdout as the only source of truth for a calculation — never to narrate a result the CLI did not actually produce:

```bash
ontograph field build "$STUDY" --poet hafez --category ghazal --json
ontograph census "$STUDY" --object mirror --json
ontograph map recurrence "$STUDY" --object mirror --unit poem --json
```

Every invocation must check the process exit code and treat a non-zero exit, a missing `ontograph` executable, or a JSON parse failure as an explicit stop condition reported to the researcher — reusing §61's existing "must fail explicitly when the engine adapter is unavailable rather than pretending that prose instructions executed a calculation," now made concrete: for Claude Code, "unavailable" means "the Bash call did not return a well-formed `OperationSpec`-tagged JSON result," full stop, regardless of how confident a prose completion might otherwise sound.

## 79. Permissioning posture

Claude Code gates Bash execution through the session's permission mode. Corpus-mutating and release-creating verbs (`field build`, `object add`, `calibrate`, `release`) should be named explicitly in the project's `.claude/settings.json` allowlist rather than granting blanket Bash access, and destructive verbs (workspace deletion, forced object merge) should stay outside any allowlist and require interactive confirmation every time — the same reversibility discipline the rest of this specification already applies to research state (never overwrite a Profile, never delete a rejected Trace) extended to the harness layer that now executes it.

## 80. v0.1 checklist mapped to this runtime

Every item in §71's minimum complete research loop maps to a concrete artifact under this binding: the Python core and CLI (§59, §62) are unchanged; "conversational skill" (§71) is precisely `.claude/skills/persian-poetry-ontograph/` above; "runtime tool adapter when the target host cannot execute the CLI" is explicitly **not built** for this target per §75; the append-only `EventRecord` log and versioned `ResearchRelease` (§71) gain the git-backed workspace convention from §60. Nothing in §71 is added or removed — this part only names, for one specific host, which of its already-listed components apply.

# Appendix B — Minimum complete study package

A study can be considered methodologically complete for its current scope when it contains:

1. Research Situation Record;
2. pinned Corpus Snapshot;
3. Field Charter and Scope Specification;
4. Seed/Object Address/Lexical Anchor history;

# Appendix C — Decision stability

The specification contains decisions with different levels of stability. This distinction prevents philosophical commitments from being confused with replaceable engineering defaults.

## C.1 Methodological invariants

These require explicit methodological justification before they change:

- a representation or Profile does not exhaust its object;
- corpus snapshot and transformation conditions travel with results;
- Seed, Object Address, Lexical Anchor, Anchor Hit, and Occurrence Assessment remain distinct;
- close and distant operations can pressure and reopen one another;
- every consequential distant result has a source-return route;
- Profile and Experiment retain different responsibilities;
- Trace precedes formal Relation-Object when relation type remains unsettled;
- relation identity can persist while description changes;
- Use-Status and Claim Permission remain separate;
- graph projections declare their relation layer;
- AI proposal remains distinguishable from evidence;
- research genesis remains replayable through versioned records and append-only events.

## C.2 Product requirements

These are strongly justified by the intended research practice, although they are not consequences of OOO alone:

- nontechnical researcher interaction;
- progressive disclosure of formulas and reductions;
- deterministic census separated from semantic retrieval;
- calibration before object-level scaling;
- stratified source-return reading sets;
- epistemic contract tests as part of QA;
- versioned Research Releases.

## C.3 Provisional engineering defaults

These may change after implementation evidence without changing the research method:

- Python as the deterministic engine language;
- SQLite as the first derived research index;
- YAML/JSONL workspace formats;
- exact CLI syntax;
- the first conservative Persian normalization and tokenizer;
- DP-family dispersion as an optional displayed measure;
- lift as an optional association view;
- exact calibration sample defaults;
- whether a browser workbench is required after conversational pilots;
- whether a target runtime uses direct CLI execution or an MCP/equivalent adapter — **settled for the Claude Code deployment specifically** in Part XIII (§75): direct CLI, no adapter. A different host may still require the adapter path; that remains provisional at the project level even though it is now decided for this one runtime.

A provisional choice becomes a project standard only after it has been tested on realistic studies and its failure conditions are understood.

## C.4 Experimental reserve

Higher-order relations, latent-factor views, spectral/community diagnostics, temporal trajectories across proxies, richer random-reference models, intermedial ontographic machines, and multi-model Profile comparisons remain available as future apparatuses. They enter the core only when a Research Situation can state what new observation they are expected to make possible.
